# Axial radial web (V2)

The existing Optimize Frame action generates a reversible exterior web proposal. It preserves the original IOS, frame, final guide, sleeves, implants, functional volumes and their materials. The existing accept/discard controls remain the only review actions.

A continuous curved design sheet references the immutable IOS and the actual upper frame, using the opposite insertion-axis direction. Its frame band adapts to the measured footprint width. Distant upper-frame origins trace monotone geodesic-distance-guided routes toward sleeve wall ports; shared wavefront crossings form transverse connections and real cells. The sheet can bridge openings. These are distance-guided exterior-surface paths, not exact IOS geodesics across missing surface and not a claimed fractal or structural topology optimization.

All sleeves participate. A pinned sleeve assignment also reaches central sleeves whose Voronoi regions do not reach the frame. Each sleeve retains its own axis and protected flat caps and drill cylinder.

The final volume is the union of exact variable-radius capsules. This preserves rounded bifurcations and taper sections. Sub-voxel fairing and bounded extraction cleanup affect only the generated candidate. New snapshots double the primary and transverse body diameters and select half the reference primary frame origins (rounded up), distributed across the frame and all sleeves. Each member has a constant radius, including its ends: there are no terminal flares. The shared frame sparse-control and centripetal Catmull-Rom helpers smooth the paths (alpha 0.5, 5 mm control spacing, 0.3 mm sample spacing), with fixed common junctions and exterior clearance correction. The native report records the actual density, radii, shared smoothing method and section checks. No new controls are added.

The isolated worker certifies a closed oriented mesh, no self intersections, expected supported components, surviving physical cells, continuous inscribed section bounds, final triangle cap/lumen/functional keepouts, no lower-frame contact, no IOS intersection or containment, and local positive-volume attachment to both original supports and the actual visible final guide. Each component must attach to the original frame and a sleeve. Missing proof fails closed.

SHA256 manifests bind exported inputs, generated volume and shared graph. Staging and acceptance require an unchanged clinical fingerprint, insertion axes, files and preview geometry. Existing confirmed reinforcements are archived reversibly on acceptance. Geometry checks do not establish mechanical or clinical suitability.


## Fair surfaces at connections

New requests also fair the geometry of branch intersections, rather than only their paths or display normals. The independently implemented one-sided heat fairing of the complete union fills concave seams while preserving the original strut envelope; fairing the union once prevents sample-dependent beads. The scale is automatically 0.85 times the smallest body radius. The native inscribed-section check still measures the extracted mesh.

At the frame and curved sleeve walls, the shared Coons-Hermite support builder constructs bounded flat transitions with zero fullness. It places the neck plane away from the support footprint and adapts the footprint and positive tangent handles to the actual frame width, checking the lower-facing frame surface before union. No extra controls are exposed. The final welded mesh must pass all original anatomy, section, sleeve and attachment gates. Tiny extraction slivers are removed only below 0.1 percent of the vertex count and four extraction voxels in extent.

Reference: Luo and Zou, Soap Film-Inspired Subdivisional Lattice Structure Construction, Computer-Aided Design (2025), DOI 10.1016/j.cad.2025.103950. Its abstract motivates fair nodal surfaces. DSG uses its own level-set and existing Coons-Hermite implementation; it does not implement PN-Loop or incorporate the authors' code.
